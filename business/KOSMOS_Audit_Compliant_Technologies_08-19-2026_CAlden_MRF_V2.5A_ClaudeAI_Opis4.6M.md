## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Compliant Technologies LLC is a Kentucky-based, veteran-owned company that manufactures and distributes the G.L.O.V.E. (Generated Low Output Voltage Emitter), a wearable conducted-energy device designed to deliver electric shocks through direct skin contact to achieve "compliance" from human subjects. The company markets this product to law enforcement, corrections, military, healthcare, and — notably — school resource officers. In August 2026, the Department of Homeland Security published a procurement notice for a sole-source, no-bid contract worth $10–$20 million to equip ICE agents with the device.

This audit applies the KOSMOS Framework — 7ES structural analysis, Fundamental Design Principle scoring, Designer Query Discriminator, and Observer Collapse Function — to evaluate Compliant Technologies as a system. The framework treats the company not as an isolated product manufacturer, but as a node within a larger compliance-technology ecosystem that converts state authority into private profit through the commodification of pain.

**Key Findings:**

The system exhibits a dominant extractive pattern across all eight Fundamental Design Principles. The Global FDP score of **0.93** (Unnatural, Collapse-Prone) reflects an architecture that concentrates benefits among a small ownership class and government procurement officials while externalizing all costs — physical pain, cardiac risk, civil rights erosion, and psychological trauma — onto populations with zero agency within the system: incarcerated people, detained immigrants, students, and psychiatric patients.

The Designer Query Discriminator of **0.80** (Unnatural) confirms that the system's extractive characteristics are designed-in features, not emergent flaws. The product's core function — inflicting pain to produce compliance — is the design, not a misuse scenario. The Observer Collapse Function of **0.72** (Critical Risk), adjusted to **0.88** (Maximum Risk) with complexity factors, indicates that the system's viability depends entirely on continued government procurement and public tolerance of pain-compliance technology on vulnerable populations.

The audit identifies a fundamental incoherence at the heart of Compliant Technologies' value proposition: the company simultaneously claims the device is too mild to cause injury while marketing it as effective enough to override human will within three seconds. These claims are mutually exclusive. A device incapable of causing physiological harm cannot produce involuntary compliance; a device capable of overriding voluntary motor control is, by definition, causing physiological harm. The company's own manual acknowledges risk factors including cardiac rhythm disruption and "sudden death."

## PHASE 1: STRUCTURAL DISSECTION — 7ES ANALYSIS

### Element 1: INPUT

**Subsystem 1.1 — Capital Inputs:** Veteran-owned small business classification provides access to SBA certification and set-aside contracting preferences. No prior federal contracts on record before the 2026 DHS procurement notice. Primary revenue appears to derive from direct sales to approximately 700+ agencies across 40 states and 9 countries, with unit pricing around $1,625 per pair.

**Subsystem 1.2 — Technological Inputs:** The CD3 (Conductive Distraction and De-escalation Device) technology was not invented domestically. Company materials indicate the technology was discovered abroad and imported for U.S. application. The specific origin remains undisclosed. Raw materials include conductive electrode pads, rechargeable battery systems delivering 324–380 volts maximum output, and standard tactical glove construction.

**Subsystem 1.3 — Legitimacy Inputs:** The system imports legitimacy through three channels: (a) veteran-owned status creating patriotic credibility; (b) framing the product as "non-lethal" and "non-injurious" despite documented death and criminal charges associated with its use; (c) endorsement from policing industry figures (Institute for the Prevention of In-Custody Deaths).

**Subsystem 1.4 — Policy Inputs:** The current immigration enforcement environment — including the Trump administration's stated goal of 2,000 arrests per day and congressional funding expansion for ICE — creates the demand environment for scaled procurement. The product becomes viable at scale only when state policy creates sufficient volume of human beings requiring "compliance."

**7ES Assessment:** Input architecture is heavily dependent on government policy creating demand for pain-compliance tools. The system does not create demand organically; it requires state violence infrastructure to function commercially.

### Element 2: OUTPUT

**Subsystem 2.1 — Primary Product Output:** The G.L.O.V.E. device — a tactical glove with concealed electrode activation delivering electric shock on skin contact. The CTG-5 model delivers 324–380 volts. Activation is via a concealed wrist button, producing what the manufacturer describes as "immediate and sharp" pain stimulus. The device is specifically designed to be visually indistinguishable from standard patrol gloves.

**Subsystem 2.2 — Pain Output:** The system's functional output is pain. All commercial value derives from the device's capacity to inflict sufficient pain to override human voluntary behavior within approximately three seconds. The manufacturer's own demonstration videos show fit, prepared officers screaming and dropping to the ground during controlled demonstrations.

**Subsystem 2.3 — Compliance Output:** The stated output is "compliance" — the cessation of resistance by the subject. This output is achieved exclusively through pain stimulus. There is no mechanism in the device that produces compliance through communication, de-escalation technique, or conflict resolution. The word "de-escalation" in the product name (Conductive Distraction and De-escalation Device) is a marketing designation, not a functional description.

**Subsystem 2.4 — Revenue Output:** Revenue flows exclusively to Compliant Technologies LLC and its ownership (founder/CEO Jeff Niklaus). The $10–$20 million ICE contract would represent a transformative revenue event for a company with 12 employees and a residential business address.

**7ES Assessment:** The system's outputs are asymmetrically distributed. Revenue and capability concentrate in the company and purchasing agencies. Pain, risk of injury, risk of death, and civil rights erosion are externalized entirely onto the populations subjected to the device — populations with zero purchasing power, zero contractual relationship with the company, and zero capacity to opt out.

### Element 3: PROCESSING

**Subsystem 3.1 — Manufacturing Processing:** Small-scale manufacturing and assembly, likely contract-manufactured given the company's 12-employee size. The core processing function is converting electrical components and tactical glove materials into a wearable pain-delivery system.

**Subsystem 3.2 — Marketing Processing:** The company's marketing processing involves systematic euphemism generation. Pain is reframed as "distraction." Shock is reframed as "stimulation." Forced compliance is reframed as "de-escalation." The product name itself — G.L.O.V.E. — uses an acronym to obscure that the device is an electric shock weapon. The company's tagline, "The New Protocol in Peacekeeping," applies the word "peace" to a device whose sole function is inflicting pain.

**Subsystem 3.3 — Training/Certification Processing:** The training certification course consists of approximately 3.5 hours of lectures and demonstrations, including breaks, followed by a multiple-choice test whose answer key is publicly posted on the company's website. Recertification is required every two years.

**7ES Assessment:** Processing is optimized for euphemism generation and liability insulation rather than harm reduction. The 3.5-hour training with a publicly-posted answer key represents minimum viable compliance theater rather than rigorous safety certification. For context, hairdressers in most U.S. states require 1,000+ hours of training before licensure.

### Element 4: CONTROLS

**Subsystem 4.1 — Internal Use Guidelines:** The company's own use guidelines prohibit use against mere "verbal defiance or belligerence" and warn against use on children, pregnant women, elderly people, and people with cardiac conditions or implanted defibrillators. The manual states a maximum recommended application of 15 seconds per deployment.

**Subsystem 4.2 — External Regulatory Controls:** The device operates in a regulatory gap. It is not FDA-regulated as a medical device despite delivering voltage through skin contact with acknowledged cardiac risk. It is not subject to the same oversight framework as Tasers. No independent peer-reviewed safety testing is publicly available. The company's primary safety claim — "no documented injuries from direct use" — is self-reported and contradicted by the Mansfield wrongful death case and criminal charges in South Carolina.

**Subsystem 4.3 — Procurement Controls:** The DHS procurement is structured as a sole-source, no-bid contract, bypassing competitive bidding. This eliminates the control mechanism of competitive evaluation, independent specification review, and alternative vendor assessment.

**7ES Assessment:** Controls are cosmetic. Internal guidelines exist on paper but have been demonstrably exceeded in documented incidents (45-second and 99-second shock applications against a 15-second maximum). External regulatory controls are essentially absent. Procurement controls have been bypassed via sole-source justification. The system operates with near-zero effective constraint on actual use.

### Element 5: FEEDBACK

**Subsystem 5.1 — Injury Reporting (Active Feedback):** The company claims "no documented injuries from direct use" — a claim that functions as feedback suppression rather than feedback processing. The Mansfield death involved combined GLOVE and Taser application, allowing the company to attribute the death to the combination rather than its device specifically. This creates a structural feedback evasion mechanism.

**Subsystem 5.2 — Event Recording (Passive Feedback):** The CTG-5 model includes event-recording capability that logs each activation. However, the data remains with the using agency, not subject to independent review, and provides no automatic override for excessive duration or frequency of application.

**Subsystem 5.3 — Market Feedback:** The company's primary feedback loop is sales volume, not harm metrics. Success is measured by agency adoption (700+ agencies, 40,000+ uses), not by outcomes for people subjected to the device. There is no documented systematic collection of outcomes data from subjected populations.

**7ES Assessment:** Feedback loops are structurally inverted. The system receives positive feedback (sales growth) precisely when harm potential expands (more agencies deploying the device on more people). Negative feedback — injury, death, civil rights violations — is structurally excluded from the company's operational feedback through attribution-evasion mechanisms and the absence of independent outcome tracking.

### Element 6: INTERFACE

**Subsystem 6.1 — Agency Interface:** The company interfaces with purchasing agencies through law enforcement trade shows, conferences (including school safety conferences), and direct sales. The interface is exclusively with institutional buyers who will never personally be subjected to the device.

**Subsystem 6.2 — Subject Interface:** The interface between the device and the human being subjected to it is skin contact delivering electric shock. The device is specifically designed to be invisible — an "invisible partner" per the company's own marketing. This design choice eliminates the subject's ability to identify the source of pain, eliminates bystander ability to witness force application, and eliminates camera/body-worn camera ability to document the triggering action.

**Subsystem 6.3 — Regulatory Interface:** Near-absent. No FDA interface for safety review. No independent testing body interface. The company self-certifies safety claims.

**7ES Assessment:** The interface architecture is the system's most revealing design feature. The deliberate invisibility of the device — designed to look like an ordinary glove — means that force can be applied without visual evidence. This creates an accountability gap that is a feature, not a bug. The company markets this invisibility as a benefit ("low optics"), explicitly acknowledging that visible force application creates accountability pressure that their product is designed to eliminate.

### Element 7: ENVIRONMENT

**Subsystem 7.1 — Political Environment:** The current U.S. political environment featuring expanded immigration enforcement, increased ICE funding, and political incentives for demonstrating aggressive enforcement creates the demand environment for scaled procurement. The product's viability at the $10–$20 million contract scale depends on this specific political configuration.

**Subsystem 7.2 — Legal Environment:** The legal environment is characterized by qualified immunity for law enforcement, limited civil rights protections for detained populations, and a regulatory gap for conducted-energy devices below Taser-level output. The Mansfield wrongful death lawsuit represents the first major legal test of the device.

**Subsystem 7.3 — Civil Society Environment:** Civil rights organizations (ACLU, National Immigration Law Center, Physicians for Human Rights) have raised immediate alarm. Multiple state officials (New York governor and attorney general) have stated that use would constitute "undue harm" and civil rights violations. Media coverage has been extensive and predominantly critical.

**7ES Assessment:** The system's environmental dependence is extreme. It requires a political environment tolerant of pain-compliance technology on vulnerable populations, a legal environment with limited accountability for force application, and suppression of civil society feedback. Environmental shifts in any of these dimensions threaten system viability.

## COUNTERFACTUALS

The MRF requires at least one counterfactual or adversarial reading per section. The following counterfactuals test the audit's findings against alternative interpretations.

### Counterfactual 1: "The device genuinely reduces injuries compared to alternatives"

**Claim:** If officers would otherwise use batons, prone restraint, or firearms, the GLOVE represents a less harmful alternative that reduces net injury to subjects and officers.

**Analysis:** This counterfactual has surface plausibility but fails on three grounds. First, it assumes a fixed quantum of force that must be applied — that the only question is which tool delivers it. This assumption treats compliance-through-violence as inevitable rather than examining whether the encounter requires force at all. Many GLOVE applications documented in media involve people exhibiting "verbal defiance" or passive resistance — categories the manufacturer's own guidelines prohibit targeting. Second, the device's designed invisibility eliminates the deterrent effect that visible force tools provide — the visible presence of a Taser or baton creates compliance incentive without application, while an invisible shock glove provides no deterrent and must be activated to function. This may increase total force applications even if individual applications are less severe. Third, the "less harmful alternative" framing depends on the company's unverified safety claims. The Mansfield death demonstrates that the device can contribute to fatal outcomes. Until independent safety testing establishes the device's actual harm profile, the "less harmful" claim is an unverified marketing assertion, not an empirical finding.

**FDP Impact if Counterfactual Accepted:** SP might increase to 2.0; AR might increase to 2.0; Global FDP would rise to approximately 1.2. The system would remain deep within the Unnatural/Collapse-Prone classification.

### Counterfactual 2: "Misuse is the officers' fault, not the product's fault"

**Claim:** The Mansfield death, the South Carolina criminal charges, and guideline violations represent misuse by individual officers, not design flaws in the product. The company provides guidelines; it cannot control individual behavior.

**Analysis:** This counterfactual mirrors the gun manufacturer's liability defense and fails for the same systemic reason. The product is designed without built-in enforcement of its own guidelines. No automatic shutoff at 15 seconds. No cumulative exposure limiter. No alarm when guidelines are exceeded. No remote monitoring. The training certification consists of 3.5 hours with a publicly-posted answer key. The company has designed a product whose misuse is predictable, has designed training insufficient to prevent misuse, and has designed no technical safeguards against misuse — then claims misuse is the user's responsibility. In systems terms, this is a Controls failure (Element 4): the system lacks the internal constraint mechanisms necessary to maintain operation within its stated parameters. A system that relies entirely on external enforcement to prevent foreseeable harm and provides no internal enforcement mechanisms has designed-in its own failure mode.

**FDP Impact if Counterfactual Accepted:** AR might increase to 2.0 (crediting the existence of guidelines even without enforcement); IH might increase to 1.0 (crediting the guidelines as intellectual honesty about limitations). Global FDP would remain well within Unnatural range.

### Counterfactual 3: "The no-bid contract is justified because the product is unique"

**Claim:** Sole-source procurement is appropriate because no other company produces an equivalent wearable conducted-energy glove, and competitive bidding would be futile.

**Analysis:** This counterfactual reveals rather than resolves the system's extractive architecture. The fact that only one company produces this specific product means that the government is creating a monopoly revenue stream for a single 12-person company — a $10–$20 million windfall — without market competition to establish fair pricing, independent specification review, or alternative safety evaluation. The sole-source justification converts the absence of competition from a market efficiency problem into a procurement feature. Moreover, the "uniqueness" argument ignores that the product category — conducted-energy compliance devices — includes competitors (Axon/Taser), and that Axon has specifically moved its newest models away from the pain-compliance contact-shock approach that the GLOVE uses, citing concerns about misuse potential. The sole-source contract does not reflect technological uniqueness; it reflects the absence of other companies willing to manufacture this specific approach to pain compliance.

### Counterfactual 4: "Veteran-owned status and patriotic mission redeem the extractive pattern"

**Analysis:** Jeff Niklaus's military service (20 years active duty, deployments in Somalia, Iraq, and Afghanistan, retired Chief Warrant Officer Four) is not in question, and this audit does not impugn his character or intentions. ### Counterfactual 5: "The school deployment shows the device works in sensitive contexts"

**Claim:** The fact that Omaha schools purchased the device and used it twice with no reported injuries demonstrates that the device can be deployed responsibly in even the most sensitive environments.

**Analysis:** This counterfactual inverts the evidence. The deployment of electric shock capability in schools where children are legally compelled to attend represents the expansion of the pain-compliance paradigm into an environment where it is most obviously inappropriate, not evidence of appropriate use. Two deployments on students (including a student who "threw a chair" — behavior routinely managed in schools without electric shock) represent the normalization trajectory of the technology. The absence of reported injury in two incidents does not establish safety; it establishes that the device has not yet caused documented harm in schools. The Mansfield death occurred in a corrections facility where the device had also been used many times "without injury" before the fatal incident. The school deployment reveals the system's expansion logic: if pain-compliance technology is acceptable in jails, then in immigration enforcement, then in schools, then in hospitals — each new context normalizes the next.

## CONCLUSION

Compliant Technologies LLC operates as a system that converts state authority into private revenue through the commodification of human pain. Its product — the G.L.O.V.E. — is an electric shock weapon whose core function is inflicting sufficient pain to override human voluntary behavior. The system's architecture concentrates all benefits (revenue, enforcement capability, political utility) among actors with zero exposure to the device's effects, while externalizing all costs (pain, injury risk, death risk, civil rights erosion, community trust destruction) onto populations with zero agency within the system.

The framework scores confirm what the system's design reveals:

| DQD | 0.60 | Unnatural (boundary) |

The system's most diagnostically significant feature is its designed invisibility. A product engineered to look like an ordinary glove — whose activation is invisible to bystanders, supervisors, and cameras — is not a de-escalation tool. It is a tool designed to deliver pain without accountability. The company's own marketing term, "low optics," is an inadvertent confession: the product is designed to reduce the visibility of state violence, not to reduce state violence.

The Mansfield case provides the empirical test of the system's claims. A man arrested for public intoxication — a non-violent offense — was shocked approximately 40 times (27 GLOVE, 13 Taser) with individual applications lasting up to 99 seconds against a 15-second guideline maximum. He died of cardiac arrest. The system produced exactly the outcome its design makes possible: a pain-delivery tool with no built-in use limits, deployed by minimally trained personnel, in an environment with inadequate oversight, on a person with no capacity to refuse or escape. This is not misuse. This is the system operating at the boundary conditions its design permits.

**Biomimetic Repair Pathway:** No natural system produces compliance through pain. Biological systems achieve coordination through distributed signaling (ant pheromone trails), mutual benefit (bee pollination), or environmental responsiveness (forest fire adaptation). The concept of "making someone compliant" through pain stimulus has no natural analog. The repair pathway for this system is not optimization of the pain-delivery mechanism but replacement of the compliance-through-pain paradigm with approaches that create mutual benefit: crisis intervention teams, mental health co-responders, de-escalation training that involves actual de-escalation (communication, empathy, environmental modification), and restorative justice frameworks that address the conditions producing non-compliance rather than punishing non-compliance through pain.

The system's Maximum Collapse Risk (OCF_adj = 0.88) reflects its dependence on a specific political configuration (expanded enforcement, tolerance of pain-compliance technology), a specific legal configuration (regulatory gap, qualified immunity, no adverse precedent), and a specific public tolerance configuration (insufficient awareness of how the technology is used). Any significant shift in these conditions — a Mansfield lawsuit ruling establishing liability, FDA regulatory action, legislative prohibition, shift in enforcement policy — could trigger rapid system collapse. The 3–12 month timeline under sustained pressure reflects the system's extreme centralization (single company, single product, single founder) and minimal adaptive capacity (no diversification, no self-correction mechanisms, no feedback integration).

*This system is unnatural. Its persistence depends on recursive belief from observers who choose not to examine what the product actually does. When observation occurs — when body cameras record, when lawsuits discover, when journalists investigate, when the public sees officers screaming and hitting the ground in demonstration videos — belief erodes. The system's collapse is a function of visibility. The irony is inescapable: a product designed to be invisible will ultimately be collapsed by becoming visible.*
