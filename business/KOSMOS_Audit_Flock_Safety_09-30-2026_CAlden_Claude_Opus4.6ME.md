## Phase 1: 7ES Structural Dissection

The 7ES framework maps Flock Safety’s system anatomy by identifying all seven elements, including hidden and shadow structures that the company does not publicly acknowledge.

### Element 1: Input

**Visible Inputs:** License plate images captured by solar-powered ALPR cameras; vehicle photographs (make, model, color, distinguishing features); GPS/location data with timestamps; taxpayer funding via municipal contracts ($2,000–$2,500/camera/year); venture capital investment ($8.4 billion total valuation from a16z, Tiger Global, Founders Fund/Peter Thiel).

**Hidden Inputs:** Biometric data (facial recognition capability described in patent but publicly denied); Philippine gig worker labor at $2–5/hour for AI model training using sensitive surveillance footage; behavioral pattern data from vehicle movement tracking across time; cross-jurisdictional data aggregation creating a de facto national surveillance network without federal authorization.

**7ES Weakness:** Input sourcing is fundamentally non-consensual. The surveilled population—every driver passing a Flock camera—provides the system’s primary input (their images, location, movement patterns) without informed consent, compensation, or practical ability to opt out. This is the foundational extraction: the raw material is harvested from public space without the knowledge or agreement of the people being harvested.

### Element 2: Output

**Visible Outputs:** “Vehicle of Interest” alerts to subscribing law enforcement agencies; historical vehicle location queries; “hot list” matching (stolen vehicles, AMBER alerts); marketing materials claiming crime reduction.

**Hidden Outputs:** Comprehensive movement-pattern databases for all scanned vehicles (not just suspects); data accessible to ICE through local police workarounds; shareholder value extraction ($8.4 billion valuation built on surveillance data); Flock-funded “research” studies with employee co-authors presented as independent validation.

**7ES Weakness:** Output asymmetry is extreme. The system produces two fundamentally different output streams: a narrow stream of crime-solving utility (the marketed product) and a massive stream of mass surveillance data, shareholder value, and externalized harm (the actual optimization target). The ratio of marketed output to actual output represents the core deception architecture.

### Element 3: Processing

**Visible Processing:** Automated license plate recognition via machine learning; vehicle classification algorithms; hot list matching against law enforcement databases.

**Hidden Processing:** Facial recognition capability (per patent documentation despite public denials); AI models trained on sensitive surveillance footage by $2–5/hour Philippine gig workers; behavioral pattern analysis from aggregate movement data; cross-jurisdictional data correlation creating movement profiles without warrants.

**7ES Weakness:** Processing is a black box. 55 documented security vulnerabilities. A reverse-engineered camera yielded 1.6 million stored images. The gap between publicly described processing (simple plate matching) and patented processing (facial recognition, comprehensive surveillance) represents a fundamental transparency failure. CBS News investigation found crime-solving claims could not be independently verified; the company’s own study had Flock employees as co-authors.

### Element 4: Controls

**Visible Controls:** Company-stated data retention policies; Terms of Service with law enforcement clients; claimed restrictions on ICE data sharing.

**Hidden Controls (or Absence Thereof):** No federal ALPR regulation exists. Company policies are self-imposed and self-enforced with no external verification. ICE accesses data through local police workarounds—the “control” against ICE access is architectural theater. Over 100 documented cases of police misuse (stalking, unauthorized queries) indicate controls on law enforcement use are non-functional. Senate hearing September 23, 2026: Flock executives *absent*—refusing even the appearance of external accountability.

**7ES Weakness:** Controls are performative. The system has no genuine constraint mechanism. Self-imposed policies exist primarily as marketing material and legal liability shields, not as operational constraints on system behavior. The absence of Flock executives from Senate testimony is diagnostic: the control subsystem collapses when actual enforcement is attempted.

### Element 5: Feedback

**Active Feedback:** 214+ city contract cancellations (market feedback); Florida revoking all ALPR permits (regulatory feedback); Texas halting state funding; LAPD, Boston, Santa Cruz, Austin, Cambridge, Fort Collins cancellations; Senate investigations; CBS News exposés; community activism.

**System Response to Feedback:** Employee buyouts rather than operational reform; continued marketing of disputed crime-reduction claims; absence from Senate hearings; no documented architectural changes in response to privacy concerns.

**7ES Weakness:** Feedback loops are broken. The system receives massive negative feedback (214+ cancellations, state-level bans, Senate hearings) but does not self-correct. Instead, it contracts (buyouts) while maintaining the same operational architecture. This is the hallmark of a system optimized for extraction rather than service: feedback that threatens the extraction mechanism is treated as noise, not signal. The system is dying rather than adapting.

### Element 6: Interface

**Visible Interfaces:** Law enforcement dashboard and API; municipal procurement processes; company website and marketing materials; public relations apparatus.

**Hidden Interfaces:** ICE access via local police workaround (a shadow interface designed to circumvent stated policy); Philippine outsourcing pipeline for AI training data (labor extraction interface hidden from public view); venture capital reporting interfaces (the actual primary interface governing system behavior); patent filings (revealing capabilities denied through public interfaces).

**7ES Weakness:** Interface deception is architectural. The system presents one interface to the public (“safety tool helping communities”) and operates through another set of interfaces entirely (mass surveillance data broker, VC return vehicle, warrantless tracking system). The ICE workaround is particularly diagnostic: the system claims no ICE interface while maintaining one through deliberate architectural indirection.

### Element 7: Environment

**Operating Environment:** Post-9/11 surveillance normalization culture; regulatory vacuum (no federal ALPR law); VC-driven growth imperative requiring expansion at all costs; policing culture favorable to technology-enabled surveillance; digital infrastructure enabling mass data collection at low marginal cost.

**Environmental Shifts:** Growing privacy awareness; community activism against surveillance; state-level regulatory action (Florida, Texas); Congressional attention (Senate hearings); journalistic investigations (CBS News, The Intercept); 2024–2026 immigration enforcement escalation increasing ICE data access pressure.

**7ES Weakness:** Environmental dependency is critical. Flock’s system was designed for a permissive environment (no regulation, low public awareness, post-9/11 surveillance tolerance). The environment is rapidly shifting toward regulation and resistance. The system has no mechanism for operating in a regulated environment because regulation would require transparency that would reveal the gap between marketed purpose and actual operation.

**7ES Structural Summary:** Every element of Flock Safety’s system anatomy exhibits extraction architecture. Inputs are harvested without consent; outputs are asymmetrically distributed; processing is opaque and deceptive; controls are performative; feedback is ignored; interfaces are duplicitous; and the environment is turning hostile. This is not a system with flaws—it is a system whose fundamental architecture is extractive.

**Formula:** SP = 10 × (Benefits to all stakeholders / Benefits to controllers)

**Analysis:** Flock Safety’s stated purpose is community safety through crime reduction. Its actual operational purpose, as revealed by patent language, financial structure, and system architecture, is surveillance data extraction for venture capital returns.

**Who benefits:** Flock shareholders and VC investors (a16z, Tiger Global, Founders Fund/Peter Thiel) capture the $8.4 billion valuation. Law enforcement agencies receive a surveillance tool funded by municipal taxpayers. Flock executives extract compensation.

**Who bears costs:** Every person whose vehicle passes a Flock camera (mass surveillance without consent). Communities of color disproportionately targeted by police using Flock data. Immigrant communities subject to ICE tracking through police data workarounds. Filipino gig workers paid $2–5/hour to train AI on sensitive surveillance footage. Taxpayers funding $2,000–2,500/camera/year for a system whose crime-reduction claims are unverifiable.

**Dominant Pattern Assessment:** >60% of system operation optimizes for surveillance data extraction and VC returns. Crime-solving outcomes, even when genuine, represent a minority output stream. Per the Dominant Pattern Priority Rule, the SP score reflects the extractive baseline. The scattered positive outcomes (individual cases solved) do not offset the architectural extraction—they demonstrate the precision of selective benefit distribution.

**Score Justification:** SP = 0.5. Benefits flow overwhelmingly to controllers (VC investors, executives, shareholders). The surveilled population bears all costs with no compensation, no consent, and no meaningful benefit distribution. Even the claimed safety benefit is disputed: CBS News found crime-solving claims unverifiable, and the company’s own study was authored by Flock employees. A score above zero acknowledges only that individual cases may have been solved using the technology; it does not indicate systemic symbiosis.

### FDP 2: Adaptive Resilience (AR) — Score: 1.0/10 (Weight: 2)

**Formula:** AR = 10 × (1 − External interventions / Autonomous processes)

**Analysis:** Adaptive resilience measures a system’s capacity to self-correct when stressed without external enforcement. Flock Safety demonstrates catastrophic adaptive failure.

- 214+ cities have cancelled contracts—the system has not self-corrected in response
- 93 cancellations in August 2026 alone—an accelerating failure, not a correcting one
- Florida revoked all ALPR permits—state-level rejection requiring no adaptation from Flock because the system simply ceased operating there
- Texas halted state funding—external financial constraint, not self-correction
- Flock response: employee buyouts (contraction) rather than architectural reform
- Senate hearing: executives absent—refusing engagement with external correction mechanisms
- 55 security vulnerabilities documented without evidence of systematic remediation

**Score Justification:** AR = 1.0. The system demonstrates near-zero autonomous self-correction. Every constraint on system behavior comes from external sources (city councils, state legislatures, courts, journalists). The system’s response to stress is contraction and denial, not adaptation. A score of 1.0 rather than 0.0 acknowledges that the company still exists and performs basic operational maintenance, but the architectural extraction pattern has not been modified in response to any feedback signal.

### FDP 3: Reciprocal Ethics (RE) — Score: 0.5/10 (Weight: 1)

**Formula:** RE = 10 × (Fair exchanges / Total exchanges)

**Analysis:** Reciprocal ethics examines whether costs and benefits are shared equitably among system participants.

- **Citizens ↔ Flock:** Citizens provide their images, location data, and movement patterns without consent or compensation. They receive surveillance exposure. This is a zero-reciprocity exchange.
- **Taxpayers ↔ Flock:** Taxpayers fund camera installation ($2,000–2,500/year/camera). They receive disputed crime-reduction claims from Flock-funded studies with Flock employee authors.
- **Filipino Workers ↔ Flock:** Workers train AI models on sensitive U.S. surveillance footage for $2–5/hour. They receive below-subsistence compensation for handling material that raises serious ethical and legal questions about cross-border data handling.
- **Police ↔ Communities:** Police receive warrantless surveillance capability. Communities receive 100+ documented cases of police abuse (stalking, wrongful stops, racial profiling).
- **Immigrants ↔ ICE:** Immigrants are tracked via local police data sharing. They receive deportation risk. Zero reciprocity.

**Score Justification:** RE = 0.5. Virtually no exchange in the Flock system is reciprocal. The system is architected for unidirectional value extraction: data flows from surveilled populations to controllers, money flows from taxpayers to shareholders, labor flows from Filipino workers to the AI training pipeline. A score of 0.5 rather than 0.0 acknowledges that police departments enter voluntary contracts (an exchange exists), though the terms overwhelmingly favor Flock.

### FDP 4: Closed-Loop Materiality (CLM) — Score: 1.0/10 (Weight: 1)

**Formula:** CLM = 10 × (Recycled outputs / Total outputs)

**Analysis:** Closed-loop materiality asks whether system outputs are recycled as inputs, creating zero waste. Flock Safety generates massive material and data waste.

- 120,000–300,000 physical cameras generating electronic waste over their lifecycle

- Data extraction from communities does not generate reciprocal community-serving outputs

**Score Justification:** CLM = 1.0. The system is fundamentally linear/extractive in its material flows. Data is harvested, monetized, and stored—never recycled as community benefit. Hardware produces waste. Revenue flows out of communities to VC investors. A score of 1.0 rather than 0.0 acknowledges minimal recycling of hardware components and some legitimate municipal tax base contribution.

### FDP 5: Distributed Agency (DA) — Score: 0.5/10 (Weight: 1)

Municipal clients have limited agency (they can cancel, which 214+ have done, but cannot control system architecture). Ownership is concentrated in VC hands. A score of 0.5 rather than 0.0 acknowledges that cities retain the power to cancel contracts—a form of distributed veto even if not distributed governance.

## Phase 3: DQD Genealogy & OCF Prognosis

### Designer Query Discriminator (DQD)

The DQD determines whether system flaws are designed-in (unnatural) or emergent (natural/hybrid).

#### DT — Designer Traceability: 0.90

Flock Safety’s system rules are highly traceable to specific designers. Founded by Garrett Langley (CEO), the company’s surveillance architecture is documented in patent filings, corporate governance documents, and VC term sheets. The system’s operational rules—data collection scope, retention policies, access controls, pricing—are all traceable to deliberate corporate decisions by identifiable individuals. Patent analysis provides particularly strong traceability: the surveillance capabilities, data processing methods, and system architecture are formally documented with named inventors.

DT = 0.90 (Highly traceable to corporate designers and VC governance)

#### GA — Goal Alignment: 0.10

Goal alignment measures the ratio of regenerative to extractive outputs. Flock Safety’s outputs are overwhelmingly extractive.

- Primary output: mass surveillance data generation and VC return optimization
- Secondary output: law enforcement surveillance tools funded by public money
- Marketed output (crime solving): unverifiable claims from self-authored studies
- Externalized outputs: privacy violation, racial profiling, community trust erosion, labor exploitation

GA = 1 − (Extractive outputs / Total outputs) = 1 − 0.90 = 0.10

#### ED — Enforcement Dependency: 0.85

Enforcement dependency measures how many system processes require external enforcement to function.

- Requires active city council contract approval and renewal (being withdrawn at scale)
- Requires legal framework permitting warrantless ALPR surveillance (under legislative challenge)
- Requires police department participation and data queries (dependent on continued political will)
- Requires absence of federal regulation (regulatory vacuum now closing)
- Requires continued VC funding in a contracting market
- System collapses immediately when any of these enforcement mechanisms are withdrawn (214+ demonstrations)

ED = 0.85 (Near-total enforcement dependency)

#### DQD Calculation

DQD = (DT + GA + ED) / 3 DQD = (0.90 + 0.10 + 0.85) / 3 DQD = 1.85 / 3 = 0.617

**Classification:** DQD 0.617 falls in the (0.6, 1\] range = **Unnatural**. The system’s flaws are designed-in, not emergent. High designer traceability (0.90) confirms these are deliberate architectural choices. Low goal alignment (0.10) confirms the system optimizes for extraction. High enforcement dependency (0.85) confirms the system cannot persist without continuous external scaffolding. This is a fully designed extraction system that will collapse when its enforcement scaffolding is withdrawn—which is currently happening.

## Counterfactuals

Per MRF v2.5A, at least one counterfactual or adversarial reading is provided per major section to stress-test findings.

### Counterfactual 1: 7ES — “The System Is Simply a Camera Company Selling Hardware”

**Adversarial Reading:** Flock is just a technology vendor selling cameras. The 7ES analysis overcomplicates a straightforward hardware-as-a-service business. Cities buy cameras, cameras read plates, plates match hot lists. The “hidden” elements are projections by the auditor.

**Framework Response:** This reading fails on the company’s own patent filings. A “simple camera company” does not file patents describing “surveillance” 93 times while branding as “safety.” A simple camera company does not describe facial recognition capabilities while publicly denying them. A simple camera company does not outsource AI training to $2–5/hour workers in the Philippines using sensitive surveillance footage. A simple camera company does not architect ICE data access through local police workarounds while denying any ICE relationship. The 7ES analysis reveals what the system *does*, not what it *claims* to do. The gap between these is the extraction architecture.

### Counterfactual 2: FDP Scoring — “Crime Solving Should Earn Higher SP and CH Scores”

**Adversarial Reading:** Flock has contributed to solving real crimes, including homicides and car thefts. These are genuine community benefits. Scoring SP at 0.5 and CH at 1.0 ignores documented cases where the technology helped public safety.

**Framework Response:** The Dominant Pattern Priority Rule addresses this directly. When >60% of operational footprint is extractive, “positive outcomes flowing to privileged subpopulations receive zero weight in the calculation because selective benefit distribution is the extraction mechanism rather than mitigation of it.” Crime-solving cases involve a tiny fraction of the 20 billion plates scanned monthly.
