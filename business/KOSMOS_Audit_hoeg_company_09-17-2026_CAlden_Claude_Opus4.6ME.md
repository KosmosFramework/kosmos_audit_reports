## Executive Summary

Hoeg & Company, Ltd. is a Pennsylvania-registered limited liability company (incorporated December 10, 2007) headquartered at 5625 Fox Valley Drive, Doylestown, PA 18902. Founded by Gregory Hoeg, the firm produces proprietary ratings of U.S. bank, credit union, and insurer operational efficiency and business quality using what it calls "Efficient Frontier Analysis" (EFA). The firm claims to rate over 99% of all U.S. banks quarterly since Q1 2010 and markets these ratings through direct subscriptions ($420/quarter to $8,000/three years), the BattleFin/AWS Marketplace ($10,000/three months), and the Datarade platform.

The central finding of this audit is a structural irony so stark it constitutes the system's defining characteristic: **Hoeg & Company rates thousands of financial institutions on operational performance using a methodology it refuses to disclose, subject to no peer review, validated by no independent party, and governed by a single individual with no external accountability mechanism.** The firm demands legibility from the institutions it evaluates while maintaining total opacity about its own analytical processes.

This is not a peripheral concern. It is the dominant extractive pattern. The system optimizes for the monetization of proprietary opacity — selling judgments derived from hidden methods to buyers who cannot verify whether those judgments are sound. The fact that input data comes from public FDIC Call Reports does not resolve this. The transformation of public data into proprietary ratings through undisclosed algorithms is the extraction mechanism itself.

## Phase 1: Structural Dissection — 7ES Analysis

### Element 1: Input

**Primary Inputs:** Publicly available FDIC Call Report data submitted by banks to regulators. This data includes balance sheet items, income statement data, regulatory capital ratios, and operational metrics. The firm states its ratings cover data from Q1 2010 through the most recently reported quarter.

**Secondary Inputs:** Greg Hoeg's proprietary analytical framework, which applies concepts borrowed from investment portfolio theory (Markowitz efficient frontier) to operational performance evaluation.

**Critical Finding:** The primary input (FDIC Call Report data) is entirely public. Any analyst, academic researcher, or competing firm can access identical data. The claimed value-add exists entirely in the Processing element — the proprietary transformation algorithms — which are undisclosed.

**Subsystem Weakness:** Input diversity is zero. The firm relies on a single data source (regulatory filings) processed through a single methodology (EFA) by a single analyst (Greg Hoeg). No alternative data streams, no qualitative inputs, no stakeholder feedback mechanisms, no environmental scanning for methodology updates based on evolving banking industry realities.

### Element 2: Output

**Primary Outputs:** Quarterly ratings of bank operational efficiency and business quality. These include individual bank scores, industry performance reports, Top 50 rankings, Watch Lists of underperforming banks, and custom consulting reports.

**Secondary Outputs:** Blog posts and LinkedIn content discussing industry trends through the EFA lens.

**Critical Finding:** Outputs are marketed as superior to traditional performance measures. The firm claims its ratings are "more accurate and insightful" and represent a "TRUE BENCHMARK" with "unique" ratings where "each rating is unique, rather than being grouped into a few broad bands." These claims have never been validated by any independent party. No published study compares EFA ratings against alternative methodologies (DEA, stochastic frontier analysis, traditional financial ratios) for predictive accuracy. The claim of superiority is self-issued and unverifiable.

**Dominant Pattern Identification (per Dominant Pattern Priority Rule):** The output optimization function is revenue extraction from proprietary opacity, not transparent financial system improvement. The outputs create information asymmetry — the firm knows its methodology, buyers do not — and monetize that asymmetry.

### Element 3: Processing

**Core Process:** The "Efficient Frontier Analysis" (EFA) methodology. The firm describes this as "similar to that used in investment portfolio theory" but adapted for evaluating bank operational performance. The specific mathematical models, variable selections, weighting schemes, normalization procedures, and scoring algorithms are entirely proprietary and unpublished.

**Critical Finding:** Processing is the system's most consequential element and its most opaque. The transformation from public input data to proprietary output ratings occurs inside a black box. This is structurally identical to the opacity the firm criticizes in the institutions it rates. Academic literature on bank efficiency measurement includes well-established methodologies — Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA), Stochastic Frontier Analysis (SFA), financial ratio analysis — all of which have been subjected to extensive peer review, comparative validation, and independent replication. Hoeg's EFA has undergone none of these validation processes.

**Subsystem Weakness:** Single-point-of-failure processing. One individual (Greg Hoeg) appears to control all methodology design, execution, and interpretation. No evidence of algorithmic auditing, bias testing, sensitivity analysis, or error detection mechanisms external to the founder's own judgment.

### Element 4: Controls

**Formal Controls:** Pennsylvania LLC registration (Company #3775273). No evidence of regulatory oversight specific to the ratings function. The firm is not registered as a Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization (NRSRO) with the SEC. No evidence of professional body certification, ethical code adherence, or industry standards compliance.

**Missing Controls:** No board of directors or advisory board identified. No conflict-of-interest policies disclosed. No methodology review committee. No data quality assurance processes disclosed. No appeals mechanism for institutions that dispute their ratings. No sunset provisions or methodology revision protocols.

**Critical Finding:** Controls are functionally nonexistent beyond basic corporate registration. The system operates with less oversight than a single-auditor CPA firm, yet produces judgments that can influence bank mergers, acquisitions, workforce reductions, and market perceptions.

### Element 5: Feedback

**Active Feedback:** None identified. No published customer reviews on major platforms. No public complaints mechanism. No methodology revision history based on predictive accuracy tracking. The firm's Datarade profile shows no customer reviews. Its Avvo attorney profile shows zero reviews.

**Passive Feedback:** The firm's continued existence since 2007 constitutes passive feedback that it has found sufficient market demand. However, persistence of a small private firm is weak evidence of methodology validity — it demonstrates only that some buyers exist, not that the product is accurate.

**Critical Finding:** Feedback loops are effectively broken. The system has no mechanism for users to challenge ratings, no public tracking of predictive accuracy (e.g., did Watch List banks actually fail?), and no systematic methodology revision based on outcomes. The firm published a "Bank Performance Patterns Leading to Failure" report, but this appears to be retrospective validation (explaining past failures) rather than prospective prediction tracking.

### Element 6: Interface

**Client Interface:** Website (hoegco.org, which blocks automated access via robots.txt), LinkedIn company page, Datarade marketplace listing, BattleFin/AWS Marketplace listing, CUInsight industry portal presence.

**Distribution Partners:** BattleFin (alternative data marketplace), AWS Data Exchange.

**Critical Finding:** The interface architecture is designed for one-way information flow: ratings flow from Hoeg to buyers. No interface exists for rated institutions to provide input, challenge findings, or access methodology documentation. The firm's own website blocks automated analysis (robots.txt), which is ironic for a firm that programmatically analyzes others' publicly filed data.

### Element 7: Environment

**Regulatory Environment:** U.S. financial services data analytics. The credit rating industry has been under increased scrutiny since the 2008 financial crisis, leading to the Dodd-Frank Act's provisions on NRSROs. However, Hoeg & Company operates outside this regulatory framework because it rates operational efficiency rather than creditworthiness. This creates a regulatory gap where the firm issues influential judgments about financial institutions without the accountability structures that govern comparable rating agencies.

**Competitive Environment:** The academic literature on bank efficiency measurement is robust and well-established, with peer-reviewed methodologies (DEA, SFA) that any researcher can replicate and validate. Hoeg competes by claiming proprietary superiority over transparent, validated alternatives.

**Critical Finding:** The environmental niche Hoeg occupies — operational efficiency ratings outside NRSRO regulation — is a regulatory blind spot that permits the monetization of unvalidated judgments about financial institutions.

## Phase 2: Ethical Benchmarking — FDP Analysis

**Domain Classification:** Financial
**Weight Vector:** ET (3), RE (2), IH (2); all others weight (1)

### FDP 1: Symbiotic Purpose (SP) — Score: 2.0

**Who benefits:** Greg Hoeg (revenue from subscriptions and consulting). Distribution partners (BattleFin, AWS) who collect platform fees. Buyers who use ratings to gain competitive intelligence on rival institutions or to identify acquisition targets.

**Who is harmed or excluded:** The 5,000+ banks being rated have no input into their ratings, no access to the methodology used to judge them, and no appeals mechanism. Bank employees whose jobs are eliminated based partly on EFA-informed "Reduction in Force" consulting (documented in Datarade case study). Communities served by banks that are downgraded by opaque ratings they cannot interrogate. The broader financial research community is denied access to methodology that could advance the field if subjected to peer review.

**Application of Dominant Pattern Priority Rule:** The system's dominant pattern is value extraction through information asymmetry. Benefits concentrate in the firm and its buyers; costs and risks are externalized to rated institutions and their stakeholders who cannot verify the judgments being made about them.

**Score Justification:** SP = 2.0. The system creates benefit for a narrow set of participants (the firm, its subscribers) while imposing judgments on a far larger population (5,000+ banks) without consent, input, or recourse. The documented case study of advising "Reduction in Force" based on EFA analysis demonstrates that the system's outputs directly affect workers and communities who have zero voice in the process.

### FDP 2: Adaptive Resilience (AR) — Score: 1.5

**Assessment:** The system shows no evidence of self-correction capability. No methodology revision history is publicly available. No error tracking, no predictive accuracy monitoring, no mechanism for incorporating new research in bank efficiency measurement. The firm has operated since 2007 with what appears to be the same core methodology, despite significant evolution in the academic literature on bank performance analysis and the fundamental changes to banking following the 2008 financial crisis, Dodd-Frank, and the 2023 regional bank failures.

**Score Justification:** AR = 1.5. A system that rates others on performance while demonstrating no visible adaptation or learning in nearly two decades of operation fails the adaptive resilience test. The methodology appears frozen while the industry it evaluates has undergone radical transformation.

### FDP 3: Reciprocal Ethics (RE) — Score: 1.0

**Assessment:** The exchange structure is fundamentally asymmetric. Hoeg & Company extracts public data filed by banks with regulators, transforms it through undisclosed methods, and sells judgments about those banks back to the marketplace — without those banks having any say in the process, any access to the methodology, or any ability to verify the accuracy of their ratings.

Banks bear all the reputational risk of ratings. They file the data that makes ratings possible. They suffer consequences when ratings are unfavorable. Yet they receive none of the revenue, have none of the control, and possess none of the methodological transparency.

**Score Justification:** RE = 1.0. The cost-benefit distribution is maximally asymmetric. The rated bear all costs, the rater captures all benefits.

### FDP 4: Closed-Loop Materiality (CLM) — Score: 2.0

**Assessment:** The firm takes public data as input and produces proprietary ratings as output. These ratings are consumed by buyers and occasionally appear in blog posts and LinkedIn content. There is no evidence that rating outputs are cycled back to improve methodology, no evidence that bank responses to ratings inform future analysis, no evidence that predictive failures are studied and incorporated. The system is linear and extractive: public data enters, proprietary judgments exit, revenue flows to the firm.

**Score Justification:** CLM = 2.0. Minimal recycling of outputs into system improvement. The waste product — incorrect or misleading ratings — is never accounted for because predictive accuracy is never tracked.

### FDP 5: Distributed Agency (DA) — Score: 0.5

**Assessment:** Decision-making authority is concentrated in a single individual. Greg Hoeg appears to be the sole designer, operator, and arbiter of the EFA methodology. No advisory board, no peer review panel, no methodology committee, no external governance of any kind has been identified. One person controls all inputs, processing decisions, output interpretation, and distribution choices. This is the most extreme centralization possible in a system that issues judgments affecting thousands of institutions.

**Score Justification:** DA = 0.5. A single individual controls all system functions. This is structural autocracy in a domain that requires distributed judgment and independent validation.

### FDP 6: Contextual Harmony (CH) — Score: 2.5

**Assessment:** The firm operates within the financial data analytics ecosystem without demonstrating material harm to its local ecological or cultural habitat. Its Doylestown, PA location is incidental to its digital operations. However, the firm's outputs — which inform acquisition decisions, workforce reduction, and competitive strategy — affect communities far from Doylestown whose banking institutions are rated without their knowledge or input. The documented "Reduction in Force" consulting engagement demonstrates direct community impact from ratings-informed decisions.

**Score Justification:** CH = 2.5. Moderate negative contextual impact through remote effects on communities whose institutions are rated and restructured based on opaque judgments.

### FDP 7: Emergent Transparency (ET) — Score: 0.0

**Assessment:** This is the audit's most consequential finding.

Verifiable Processes: The firm discloses that it uses FDIC Call Report data and applies "Efficient Frontier Analysis." This constitutes approximately 10% of the information needed to evaluate methodology validity (input source disclosure only).

Withheld Data: The specific EFA algorithms, variable selection, weighting methodology, normalization procedures, scoring thresholds, sensitivity parameters, and validation protocols are entirely withheld. Estimated withheld percentage: 90%.

**ET Calculation:**
ET = (10 × 0.10) − (2 × 90) = 1.0 − 180 = −179

**Per MRF v2.5A instruction:** If FDP scores are < 0, cap the score at zero.

**ET = 0.0**

**Score Justification:** The firm that rates financial institutions on operational performance maintains near-total opacity about its own operational methodology. This is not incidental — it is the business model. The opacity is the product. If the methodology were disclosed, any competent analyst could replicate it using the same public data. Proprietary opacity is therefore the revenue-protection mechanism, not a necessary business practice.

### FDP 8: Intellectual Honesty (IH) — Score: 1.0

**Assessment:** The firm makes the following claims without providing evidence:
- Ratings are "more accurate and insightful than traditional performance measures" — no comparative validation published.
- EFA provides a "TRUE BENCHMARK" — no definition of truth criteria, no independent verification.
- Ratings "accurately identify best and worst performers" — no published predictive accuracy tracking.
- EFA is "radically new, yet incredibly simple" — no methodology disclosure permits evaluation of either novelty or simplicity.
- The impact of industry and economic cycles "has been eliminated" from ratings — an extraordinary statistical claim made without any published mathematical justification.

Hidden trade-offs include: the inherent limitations of any single-methodology approach, the potential for systematic bias in proprietary algorithms, the risk of overfitting to historical patterns, the impossibility of validating ratings without methodology access, and the conflict of interest in selling ratings to parties who may benefit from favorable or unfavorable results for specific institutions.

**Score Justification:** IH = 1.0. Approximately 90% of relevant trade-offs, limitations, and methodological uncertainties are hidden behind proprietary claims of superiority.

## Phase 3: Genealogy and Prognosis — DQD and OCF

### Designer Query Discriminator (DQD)

**Designer Traceability (DT) = 0.90**

The system is almost entirely traceable to a single designer. Greg Hoeg developed the EFA methodology, founded the company, serves as CEO, and appears to operate the system. This is among the highest DT scores possible — the system is one person's intellectual product monetized as a commercial enterprise. There is no emergent, collaborative, or evolutionary design origin.

**Goal Alignment (GA) = 0.15**

Goal Alignment measures the ratio of closed-loop (regenerative) to extractive processes. The system's primary process is extractive: taking public data, transforming it through hidden methods, selling proprietary judgments. Minimal regenerative activity is identifiable — the blog provides some public insight, and industry analysis has marginal public benefit. But the core commercial operation extracts value from information asymmetry rather than creating mutual benefit.

GA = 1 − (Extractive outputs / Total outputs) = 1 − 0.85 = 0.15

**Enforcement Dependency (ED) = 0.85**

The system requires continuous external enforcement for persistence. Without subscribers willing to pay for ratings they cannot verify, the system generates zero revenue. Without the legal framework protecting proprietary trade secrets, the methodology would be subjected to competitive analysis and potentially replicated. Without the FDIC's data collection and publication system, the firm would have no inputs. Every critical process depends on external structures the firm did not create and does not control.

**DQD = (0.90 + 0.15 + 0.85) / 3 = 0.63**

**DQD Classification: Unnatural (> 0.6)**

The system is a designed artifact — one person's intellectual construct monetized through commercial structures that depend entirely on external enforcement (intellectual property law, market demand, regulatory data infrastructure).

### Observer's Collapse Function (OCF)

**Baseline OCF Parameters:**

**B_R (Recursive Belief Factor) = 0.85**

The vast majority of system nodes require belief in EFA methodology validity. Subscribers must believe ratings are accurate without ability to verify. Distribution partners (BattleFin, AWS) must believe the data product has market value. Rated institutions' stakeholders must treat ratings as meaningful information. The entire system rests on belief in an unverifiable proprietary methodology.

**D_C (Observer Dependency) = 0.85**

The system requires conscious participation at every stage. Greg Hoeg must actively maintain and apply the methodology. Subscribers must actively pay and consume ratings. Distribution platforms must actively list and market the product. Remove any of these conscious participants and the system ceases to function.

**T_S (Intrinsic Stability) = 1.0**

The system has zero intrinsic stability without observers. If all subscribers canceled, all distribution partners delisted, and Greg Hoeg stopped producing ratings, nothing would persist. There is no physical infrastructure, no self-sustaining process, no momentum-carrying mechanism. The ratings have no value independent of the belief that they are accurate.

**OCF_base = (B_R × D_C) / T_S = (0.85 × 0.85) / 1.0 = 0.72**

**Classification: Critical Collapse Risk (0.6–1.0 range)**

## Counterfactuals

### Counterfactual 1: "The methodology is proprietary because it has genuine competitive value"

**Steelman:** All firms protect intellectual property. Hoeg's EFA may represent genuine innovation that deserves trade secret protection. Publishing the methodology would allow competitors to replicate it for free, destroying the firm's business model.

**Framework Response:** This argument confuses commercial interest with systemic legitimacy. Trade secret protection is a legal right, not evidence of analytical validity. The entire academic field of bank efficiency measurement operates on published, peer-reviewed, replicable methodologies. Hoeg's refusal to subject EFA to any validation process means buyers are purchasing unverified claims of superiority. The argument amounts to: "Trust us, but we won't show you why you should." In any other domain — medicine, engineering, food safety — an unvalidated proprietary methodology making superiority claims would be viewed with deep suspicion.

### Counterfactual 2: "The firm is small and harmless — this audit overstates the risk"

**Steelman:** Hoeg & Company is a tiny firm with limited market influence. Its ratings do not have the systemic impact of Moody's or S&P. The audit's language of "extraction architecture" and "collapse risk" is disproportionate to the actual stakes.

**Framework Response:** The KOSMOS framework audits systems as they are, not relative to larger systems. The firm's own claims establish the scope of its ambitions — it rates 99% of all U.S. banks and issues Watch Lists of institutions it deems at risk of failure. It consults on workforce reductions and business divestitures. The small scale of the firm makes its structural deficiencies *worse*, not better, because a single point of failure (the founder) has no redundancy. Additionally, the firm's distribution through AWS Marketplace and BattleFin places its ratings alongside data products from much larger providers, lending credibility by association.

### Counterfactual 3: "Using public FDIC data ensures accuracy because the inputs are reliable"

**Steelman:** The input data comes from regulated filings that have been audited and verified. The quality of inputs constrains the quality of outputs. Sound inputs make sound outputs more likely.

**Framework Response:** Sound inputs processed through unsound methods produce unsound outputs. The entire history of quantitative finance demonstrates that identical input data can produce contradictory conclusions depending on methodology. Two analysts using the same FDIC Call Report data could reach opposite conclusions about the same bank's operational efficiency depending on their variable selection, normalization, and weighting choices. Input quality is necessary but not sufficient for output quality. Methodology validity is the critical link, and methodology is exactly what Hoeg withholds.

### Counterfactual 4: "The firm has survived since 2007, which proves the methodology works"

**Steelman:** Market survival is the ultimate test. If EFA ratings were inaccurate, customers would stop paying. Nineteen years of operation constitutes strong market validation.

**Framework Response:** Market survival proves only that sufficient demand exists, not that the product is accurate. Astrology services, homeopathy providers, and sports prediction firms also survive for decades. The relevant question is not "do people buy this?" but "does it predict better than alternatives?" Without published comparative validation, survival demonstrates only the persistence of buyer belief — which is exactly what the OCF measures. The system persists because observers believe it has value, not because that belief has been verified.

### Counterfactual 5: "The founder is also a licensed attorney, which adds credibility"

**Steelman:** Gregory Hoeg appears in legal directories as a Pennsylvania attorney. Legal training and licensing adds professional credibility and ethical accountability.

**Framework Response:** Legal training and licensure are relevant to legal practice, not to financial analytics methodology validation. A law license does not substitute for peer review in quantitative finance. The Avvo profile shows zero reviews and no documented specialization in financial analytics or statistical methodology. This is not a criticism of legal expertise — it is a category observation that professional credentials in one domain do not validate claims in another.

## Conclusion

Hoeg & Company, Ltd. represents a precise case study in what the KOSMOS framework calls an "opacity monetization architecture" — a system that generates revenue by maintaining asymmetric access to information. The structural irony is acute: a firm that evaluates others' operational performance while refusing to disclose its own operational methodology embodies the very dysfunction it claims to diagnose.

The system is Unnatural across all three quantitative metrics: Global FDP of 1.73 (Collapse-Prone), DQD of 0.74 (Unnatural), and OCF of 0.85 (Maximum Collapse Risk). These scores are not harsh for the sake of harshness — they follow directly from documented evidence applied through framework formulas. A system with zero emergent transparency, near-zero distributed agency, single-person dependency for all critical functions, no peer review, no feedback loops, and no external governance simply cannot score well on metrics designed to evaluate systemic health.

The most instructive finding for the broader KOSMOS research program is the relationship between the firm's commercial model and its systemic fragility. Because all value resides in one person's undisclosed methodology, the system has no mechanism for surviving that person's withdrawal. There is no institutional knowledge, no published framework, no trained successor pool, no documented methodology that others could continue. The proprietary opacity that generates revenue also prevents institutional resilience.

**Biomimetic Repair Protocol:**

The path from Unnatural to Hybrid or Natural would require:

1. **Publish the EFA methodology** in a peer-reviewed journal, enabling independent validation, replication, and improvement. This directly addresses ET (currently 0.0) and IH (currently 1.0).

2. **Establish an independent advisory board** with expertise in quantitative finance, statistical methodology, and banking regulation. This addresses DA (currently 0.5) and Controls (currently 0.5).

3. **Implement predictive accuracy tracking** with public reporting. Track whether Watch List banks actually fail, whether top-rated banks actually outperform. This addresses AR (currently 1.5) and Feedback (currently 0.5).

4. **Create an appeals and engagement mechanism** for rated institutions. Allow banks to respond to their ratings, submit corrections, and access methodology documentation. This addresses RE (currently 1.0) and SP (currently 2.0).

5. **Develop succession and continuity planning.** Document the methodology in sufficient detail that it could survive the founder's departure. This addresses OCF collapse risk.

These interventions follow biomimetic principles: the most resilient natural systems (coral reefs, mycorrhizal networks, flocking birds) achieve durability through distributed knowledge, transparent signaling, feedback integration, and redundancy. Hoeg & Company currently lacks all of these qualities.

The system is not condemned. It is diagnosed. The severity of diagnosis serves the possibility of healing.
