## Phase 1: Structural Dissection (7ES Framework)

### Element 1: Inputs
TCEQ processes multiple input categories revealing systemic capture:

**Primary Inputs:**
- Permit applications from industrial facilities (oil refineries, chemical plants, concrete batch plants, waste sites)
- Environmental complaints from Texas residents (24/7 submission system)
- Federal environmental standards from EPA
- Scientific data on pollution and health impacts
- Public comments on permitting decisions
- Governor appointments for commissioners

**Hidden Inputs (Regulatory Capture):**
- Industry lobbying and campaign contributions to appointing governor
- Industry-designated "confidential" documents that determine permit conditions
- Self-reported emissions data from regulated entities with minimal verification
- Economic development pressure from state political leadership

**Input Subsystems Analysis:**
The dual-track input structure creates systematic bias. Industry inputs are processed through expedited channels (Permit by Rule allowing expansion without safety demonstration), while community inputs face procedural barriers (1-mile standing requirement, English-only public notices until 2020, notarized affidavits required for complaints). This asymmetry is not incidental—it is architectural.

### Element 2: Outputs
TCEQ generates outputs that systematically benefit industry while externalizing health costs onto vulnerable populations:

**Regulatory Outputs:**
- Air quality permits (including for facilities in cumulative pollution zones)
- Water discharge permits  
- Waste management authorizations
- Enforcement notices (0.5% of "upset" emission events receive any action)
- Compliance ratings for regulated entities
- Dam safety oversight

**Actual Outputs to Environment:**
- Approximately 17,000 deaths annually from outdoor air pollution in Texas
- Concentrated pollution in Black, Latino, and low-income communities
- Ethylene oxide emissions exceeding safe levels (documents on this withheld)
- Thousands of unremediated "upset" emission events

**Social Outputs:**
- Three concurrent EPA civil rights investigations (2021-present)
- Systematic exclusion of non-English speakers from participation
- Public distrust documented in Sunset Review
- Environmental justice communities organizing for agency abolition

**Output Subsystems:**
The critical finding: TCEQ's primary output is not environmental protection but industrial facilitation. Permits flow; enforcement does not. The agency produces regulatory legitimacy as a commodity for industry consumption.

### Element 3: Processing
TCEQ's processing mechanisms reveal capture at every transformation point:

**Permitting Process:**
- Staff-level permit drafting occurs before public input
- "Permit by Rule" allows expansion without case-by-case review
- Environmental justice impacts explicitly excluded from review
- Industry can designate documents "confidential" to hide pollution data
- Cumulative impacts not assessed

**Enforcement Process:**
- Thousands of "upset" events reported annually
- 0.5% receive enforcement action
- Penalty calculations allow industry to offset fines through self-selected environmental projects
- Average penalties below economic benefit of violation
- Commissioners delegate most decisions to staff, avoiding political accountability

**Complaint Processing:**
- Requires notarized affidavits for evidentiary value
- Drone footage classified as "illegally gathered" and excluded
- Investigations closed without action if minor technical criteria unmet
- No translation services until 2020 EPA agreement

**Processing Subsystems:**
TCEQ operates three distinct processing modes: permissive for industry, punitive for communities, theatrical for public legitimacy. The Sunset Review's finding that commissioners are "reluctant to regulate industry" and "encourage self-governance" confirms processing bias is systemic, not incidental.

### Element 4: Controls
TCEQ's control mechanisms enable rather than constrain pollution:

**Formal Controls:**
- Federal Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, RCRA compliance requirements
- Texas Water Code enforcement authority
- Commissioner voting on major permits and enforcement
- Legislative appropriations and Sunset Review
- EPA oversight and potential funding withdrawal

**Actual Control Function:**
- Affirmative defense allows companies to escape violations if they claim "upset" was unintentional
- One-mile standing rule excludes most affected community members from contested hearings
- Industry self-reporting with minimal verification
- Commissioners appointed by governor receiving industry campaign support
- Penalty caps ($25,000/day) below cost of compliance for major polluters

**Control Subsystems:**
The 2022 Sunset Review recommendation to increase fines to $40,000/day (rejected—stayed at $25,000) reveals control inversion: the system constrains environmental protection more effectively than it constrains pollution. Controls operate as permits to pollute, not limits on harm.

### Element 5: Feedback
TCEQ exhibits catastrophic feedback failure:

**Active Feedback (Explicit Correction Signals):**
- EPA objections to permits (repeatedly ignored)
- Public complaints (thousands annually, minimal response)
- Civil rights investigations (TCEQ withdrew from negotiations in 2023)
- Sunset Review criticisms (minimal implementation)
- Scientific evidence of health harm (rejected—NASA data after Hurricane Harvey)
- Death statistics (17,000 annually from air pollution—no systemic response)

**Passive Feedback (Viability Signals):**
- Loss of federal legitimacy (three EPA civil rights investigations)
- Community withdrawal from participation (agency seen as adversarial)
- Political controversy over environmental racism
- Staff report finding commissioners "reluctant to regulate"

**Feedback Pathology:**
TCEQ demonstrates active anti-feedback architecture. The agency doesn't fail to receive correction signals—it systematically rejects them. When NASA offered free air quality data collection after Hurricane Harvey, TCEQ refused, stating it would be "confusing." When communities provide evidence of harm, TCEQ requires notarized affidavits and excludes drone footage. When EPA investigates civil rights violations, TCEQ withdraws from negotiations.

The system is not blind to its failures. It is hostile to correction.

TCEQ's interfaces with stakeholders reveal asymmetric permeability:

**Environmental Embedding:**
TCEQ is embedded in a political-economic environment that rewards industry facilitation and punishes rigorous environmental protection. The agency responds to this environment by aligning with power (industry, governor) and resisting accountability (communities, EPA, scientific data).
