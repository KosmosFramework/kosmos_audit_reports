# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Texas State Board of Education (SBOE) is a constitutionally mandated, 15-member elected body responsible for setting curriculum standards, approving instructional materials, and managing the $65.7 billion Permanent School Fund for Texas's 5.5 million public school students. This audit applies the KOSMOS analytical framework (7ES, FDP, DQD, OCF) to evaluate the SBOE as an operational system.

The audit finds that the SBOE exhibits a dominant extractive pattern. The system optimizes for ideological transmission and donor interest alignment rather than educational quality or student benefit. Despite its constitutional democratic mandate, the operational architecture reveals systematic rejection of scientific consensus, undisclosed financial influence over curriculum development, unilateral management of billions in public funds without board consultation, and consistent disregard for overwhelming public feedback.

# PHASE 1: STRUCTURAL DISSECTION (7ES Analysis)

## Element 1: Input

The SBOE receives inputs from multiple sources, but the weighting of these inputs reveals the system's actual optimization function.

**Formal Inputs:** Legislative mandates form the primary legal input stream. Recent examples include HB 1605 (2023, literary works lists), SB 24 (2025, communist regime education requirements), and HB 27 (2025, social studies graduation requirement revisions). These inputs arrive pre-shaped by a legislature where the same donor networks that influence SBOE races also shape legislation. Texas has no individual contribution limits for state races, creating an unregulated input channel from wealth to policy.

**Hidden Inputs:** Federal tax filings revealed that the Texas Public Policy Foundation (TPPF) awarded $70,000 to the Texas Center at Schreiner University specifically for "development of Texas Essential Knowledge Skills (TEKS) Standards." The director of that center, Donald Frazier, was simultaneously serving as one of nine SBOE-appointed content advisers guiding the 2026 social studies curriculum overhaul. When Democratic board members requested investigation, Republican chair Aaron Kinsey stated: "I don't know anything about any private contracts... Nor do I anticipate giving information." This represents undisclosed financial input directly shaping curriculum output.

**Feedback-as-Input Failure:** Public testimony functions as a formal input but operationally functions as theater. Over 12,000 Texans submitted emails opposing the Bluebonnet Learning curriculum. Dozens of parents, teachers, and community advocates testified against it. Religious studies scholars warned against it. The board approved it 8-7. Over 90 members of the public testified on the 2026 social studies revision, with the majority citing glaring omissions and lack of diverse perspectives. The board proceeded without changes to its rushed timeline.

**Subsystem Weakness:** The input subsystem is privilege-calibrated. Wealthy donors, think tanks, and organized religious lobbying organizations have direct access channels to board members and content adviser appointment processes. Public testimony is formally permitted but functionally irrelevant to outcomes. This creates an asymmetric input architecture where financial inputs determine outputs while democratic inputs provide legitimacy cover.

## Element 2: Output

**Curriculum Standards (TEKS):** The system's primary output is the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills standards that govern what 5.5 million students learn. The 2026 social studies TEKS revision demonstrates the output's ideological character. Content advisers were described as a "hyper-partisan panel" dominated by right-wing political figures and ideologues, almost none of whom had K-12 classroom experience in Texas. The resulting drafts radically refocused learning on "Western civilization" while treating non-European history as peripheral. In 11th-grade U.S. history, the Great Depression was reduced from 11 standards to one. African-American and Mexican-American contributions faced systematic reduction. SBOE member Tiffani Clark stated: "You cannot cut African-American or Mexican-American contributions from the curriculum and still claim the founding documents were meant for all students."

**Textbook Approvals/Rejections:** In November 2023, the board rejected 7 of 12 eighth-grade science textbooks, primarily because they presented evolution and climate change in alignment with scientific consensus. A board member from McGraw-Hill's biology textbook removal praised the decision as a way to "stop science textbooks from making a monkey out of you and your kids." Republican board member Will Hickman suggested climate change solutions should be discussed in social studies rather than science classes.

**Bluebonnet Learning:** The TEA-developed, Bible-infused elementary curriculum was approved in November 2024 on an 8-7 vote. Governor Abbott appointed Leslie Recine to temporarily fill a vacant seat specifically for this vote, bypassing Democrat Tiffany Clark who had already been elected to serve in that role. Multiple analyses found the reading curriculum skews heavily in favor of Christianity compared to other religions. Following implementation, 4,200 errors were identified, requiring taxpayer-funded corrections.

**PSF Investment Decisions:** In March 2024, SBOE Chair Aaron Kinsey unilaterally divested $8.5 billion from BlackRock without notifying board colleagues, citing anti-ESG ideology. His own staff warned of likely increased costs and lower returns. Texas AFT noted this left 16% of the PSF "languishing in a holding pattern rather than earning additional monies" for Texas schools, during a period of extreme school funding shortages leading to mass layoffs and school closures.

## Element 3: Processing

The SBOE's processing subsystem transforms inputs into outputs through mechanisms that systematically filter for ideological alignment over educational quality.

**Content Adviser Selection:** Board members individually appoint content advisers who guide TEKS revisions. The 2026 social studies advisers were dominated by individuals with conservative political affiliations rather than K-12 classroom expertise. This selection mechanism ensures that processing is pre-filtered for ideological output before work groups even begin deliberation.

**Committee Deliberation:** The November 2023 science textbook review demonstrated processing dynamics. During committee of the whole, evolution and climate change were misrepresented as scientifically controversial. Textbooks presenting mainstream science were removed from the approved list. A textbook containing an "entire chapter on primate evolution" showing humans as members of the great ape family was rejected unanimously, despite the content representing established biological science.

**Rushed Timelines:** The 2026 social studies revision operated on an accelerated timeline despite handling standards that will govern education for the next decade. Public and expert calls to slow the process were ignored. Work groups were told they could "cut content or add content, but not both," constraining processing to predetermined parameters.

**Shadow Processing:** The $70,000 TPPF grant to a content adviser represents processing that occurs outside formal channels. When the connection was raised, the Republican majority objected to even discussing it, and the chair refused to provide information. This indicates that some processing occurs through undisclosed channels between think tanks and advisers before formal SBOE deliberation begins.

## Element 4: Controls

**Constitutional Mandate:** The Texas Constitution (Article VII, Section 8) establishes the SBOE, providing the system's foundational control. This control is robust and provides high intrinsic stability.

**Governor Control:** The governor appoints the SBOE chair from among elected members (requiring Senate confirmation), appoints the Commissioner of Education who runs TEA, and can fill vacant board seats. Governor Abbott's appointment of Leslie Recine specifically to secure the Bluebonnet vote demonstrates how gubernatorial control operates as an override mechanism against democratic outcomes.

**Legislative Control:** The Texas Legislature has progressively shifted authority from the elected SBOE to the appointed Commissioner and TEA. Senate Bill 6 shifted textbook selection authority to local districts. SB 1232 created the Texas Permanent School Fund Corporation. This fragmentation of authority dilutes the nominally democratic character of the elected board while consolidating power in appointed positions controlled by the governor.

**Partisan Supermajority Control:** The 11-4 Republican majority operates as a de facto control mechanism where partisan loyalty overrides evidence, expert testimony, and constituent feedback. The 8-7 Bluebonnet vote and 9-6 social studies TEKS votes consistently follow partisan lines regardless of substance.

**Missing Controls:** The system lacks conflict-of-interest disclosure requirements for content advisers, independent oversight of PSF management decisions, accountability mechanisms for curriculum errors, and meaningful enforcement of public input incorporation.

## Element 5: Feedback

**Active Feedback (Dysfunctional):** Public comment periods exist but function as legitimacy theater. The pattern is consistent: thousands of comments are submitted, experts testify, the board votes along predetermined partisan lines. Elections occur every two years, but Texas's no-limit campaign finance system, low voter turnout for SBOE races, and gerrymandered districts ensure that electoral feedback is captured by organized donor networks rather than responsive to general public interest.

**Passive Feedback (Ignored):** Texas ranks 40th nationally in per-pupil education spending. The state is one of only six that does not use Next Generation Science Standards. Bluebonnet Learning's 4,200 errors represent a passive feedback signal (system dysfunction confirmed by operational results) that was ignored until it became publicly embarrassing. The absence of systematic error tracking or correction mechanisms means passive feedback rarely enters processing.

**Feedback Loop Failure:** The system exhibits what the framework identifies as feedback-as-decoration: feedback mechanisms exist formally but have no functional connection to processing or output modification. The system's continued existence (passive feedback) is misinterpreted as validation of its operations rather than recognized as constitutional inertia.

## Element 6: Interface

**SBOE-TEA Interface:** The TEA, headed by the governor-appointed Commissioner, implements SBOE policies. When TEA developed Bluebonnet Learning, it acted as both publisher and regulator, a conflict that blurred the boundary between policy-setting and implementation. TEA's publishing role created a situation where "government overreach" (as multiple testifiers described it) was embedded in the interface itself.

**SBOE-Think Tank Interface (Partially Hidden):** The TPPF-to-content-adviser funding pipeline represents a hidden interface between ideological organizations and curriculum development. The chair's refusal to investigate or even acknowledge this connection indicates that the interface is deliberately maintained outside formal transparency requirements.

**SBOE-Religious Organization Interface:** Texas Values and similar faith-based organizations maintain direct interface with board members, providing testimony, endorsements, and framing that shapes board deliberation. The Bluebonnet curriculum's Christian emphasis reflects this interface's influence on output.

**SBOE-Fossil Fuel Industry Interface:** Board member statements suggesting students should learn about "the benefits of burning fossil fuels" in science class, and the chair's anti-ESG BlackRock divestment, indicate an interface between fossil fuel industry interests and both curriculum and investment decisions. Texas is the nation's largest oil and gas producing state.

## Element 7: Environment

**Political Environment:** Texas operates under unified Republican government with supermajorities in both legislative chambers. This environment eliminates external checks on SBOE partisan behavior and enables legislative authority transfers away from the elected board when convenient.

**Demographic Environment:** Texas is a majority-minority state with 5.5 million public school students from diverse racial, ethnic, religious, and linguistic backgrounds. The SBOE's output (Western-centric curriculum, Christian-dominant religious content, erasure of minority contributions) is systematically misaligned with this demographic reality.

**Economic Environment:** The oil and gas industry dominates Texas economics and politics. Anti-ESG legislation (SB 13, 2021) has been estimated to cost Texas taxpayers $270 million or more. The SBOE chair's $8.5 billion BlackRock divestment was framed as protecting the oil and gas economy, but the fund exists to serve students, not industry.

**National Education Environment:** Texas's curriculum decisions historically influenced national textbook markets due to state size and centralized purchasing. While this influence has diminished since local districts gained more selection authority, the SBOE's decisions still carry national signaling effects, particularly regarding evolution, climate change, and religious content in public education.

## FDP 1: Symbiotic Purpose (SP) — Score: 1.5

**Formula:** SP = 10 x (Benefits to all stakeholders / Benefits to controllers)

**Who Benefits:** Ideological organizations (TPPF, Texas Values), fossil fuel industry (climate change denial in curriculum, anti-ESG investment decisions), religious organizations (Bluebonnet Bible-infused curriculum), partisan operatives (control over what 5.5 million children learn), and conservative donor networks (no contribution limits, direct policy influence).

**Who Is Harmed:** Students receive ideologically filtered education that misrepresents scientific consensus, erases minority contributions, and privileges one religion over all others. Non-Christian students face isolation and bullying risk from Bible-infused curriculum. Teachers are constrained from teaching established science and comprehensive history. Taxpayers fund error corrections (4,200 in Bluebonnet alone), inefficient fund management, and ideologically motivated divestment. Communities in a majority-minority state receive curriculum that systematically underrepresents their histories and contributions.

**Dominant Pattern Priority Rule Application:** The system imposes educational degradation on the majority (5.5 million diverse students) while concentrating ideological benefits among a minority with proximity to political power. The system's architecture cannot achieve its stated purpose (quality public education for all) under current operational parameters. SP must reflect the extractive baseline.

**Adversarial Reading (Nader):** If a corporation rejected scientific consensus to serve industry donors, denied its customers accurate information, and used a last-minute board seat appointment to override consumer opposition, we would recognize it immediately as a consumer protection violation. The SBOE performs the identical function with children as the unprotected consumers.

**Score Justification:** More than 10% of the affected population (non-Christian students, minority students, students in science-dependent career tracks) experiences measurable educational harm. Per audit parameters, SP must be no greater than 3. Given the systematic nature of the harm across science, social studies, and religious content, SP = 1.5.

## FDP 2: Adaptive Resilience (AR) — Score: 2.0

**Formula:** AR = 10 x (1 - External interventions / Autonomous processes)

The SBOE demonstrates minimal self-correction capacity. The Bluebonnet curriculum was approved with 4,200 errors that reached classrooms before correction. When errors were identified, the system had no accountability mechanisms. TEA official Colin Dempsey acknowledged that "State Board of Education rules do not specify accountability measures when the board finds errors in state publishing materials." The board approved error corrections on a 9-6 partisan vote, meaning even correcting factual errors was contested.

The system requires external intervention for correction: legislative action (SB 6 shifted textbook authority), court rulings (federal courts have repeatedly blocked creationist curriculum), media exposure (national attention forced Bluebonnet error acknowledgment), and advocacy group monitoring (Texas Freedom Network, NCSE). Without these external pressures, the system demonstrates no autonomous error correction.

**Adversarial Reading (Scott):** The SBOE's processing architecture eliminates the local knowledge that teachers, historians, and scientists possess, replacing it with centralized ideological standards imposed on 1,200+ school districts. This is the "seeing like a state" problem applied to education: high-modernist simplification that destroys the diversity and expertise needed for system health.

**Score Justification:** AR = 10 x (1 - 0.80) = 2.0. Approximately 80% of corrections require external intervention.

## FDP 3: Reciprocal Ethics (RE) — Score: 1.0

**Formula:** RE = 10 x (Fair exchanges / Total exchanges)

**Exchange Analysis:** Students contribute compliance, attendance, and test performance. In return, they receive curriculum that misrepresents evolution, understates climate change, erases minority contributions, privileges one religion, and was developed with undisclosed industry funding. Teachers contribute professional expertise and classroom labor. In return, their expert testimony is systematically ignored, their academic freedom is constrained, and they are placed in positions that "conflict with ethical standards and the sanctity of the teaching profession" (Texas AFT). Taxpayers contribute funding through the PSF and general revenue. In return, $8.5 billion is divested without board consultation, anti-ESG ideology costs $270 million+, and 4,200 curriculum errors require taxpayer-funded corrections. Think tanks contribute $70,000 in undisclosed funding. In return, they receive direct influence over standards governing 5.5 million students' education for the next decade.

**Adversarial Reading (Baldwin):** Who is the curriculum for? When 11th-grade U.S. history reduces the Great Depression from 11 standards to one, when African-American contributions are systematically cut, when a majority-minority state's children are told Western civilization is the primary story worth learning, the curriculum serves those who find the full truth inconvenient, not those who need it to understand their own country.

**Score Justification:** RE = 10 x (0.10) = 1.0. Approximately 10% of exchanges approach reciprocity (PSF distributions do reach schools, some approved textbooks are adequate).

## FDP 5: Distributed Agency (DA) — Score: 1.5

**Formula:** DA = 10 x (1 - Centralized decisions / Total decisions)

Decision-making in the SBOE is concentrated through multiple reinforcing mechanisms. Public participation is formally available but functionally excluded from decision-making. Texas's no-limit campaign finance system ensures that financial power, not constituent numbers, determines who serves and how they vote.

**Constraint Type 9 Override:** The fact that SBOE members are elected does not establish distributed agency. Applying the substitution test: if an appointed corporate board made identical decisions (ignoring scientific consensus, using undisclosed industry funding to shape policy, overriding customer feedback, making unilateral billion-dollar investment decisions), we would immediately identify concentrated agency. The electoral mechanism changes nothing about the operational concentration of power.

**Score Justification:** DA = 10 x (1 - 0.85) = 1.5. Approximately 85% of consequential decisions are centrally determined.

# PHASE 3: GENEALOGY AND PROGNOSIS (DQD / OCF)

## Designer Query Discriminator (DQD)

### DT (Designer Traceability) — Score: 0.85

The SBOE's rules, standards, and operational procedures are extensively traceable to specific designers. The Texas Constitution establishes the board. The Texas Education Code specifies its authority. Individual legislators author bills (HB 1605, SB 24, HB 27) that direct SBOE action. Governor Abbott's appointment decisions are documented. Board members' votes are recorded. Content advisers are named. The TPPF's funding of TEKS development is documented in federal tax filings. This high traceability confirms the system is designed, not emergent.

DT = 0.85

### GA (Goal Alignment) — Score: 0.25

GA = 1 - (Extractive outputs / Total outputs)

The system's outputs overwhelmingly serve extraction rather than mutual benefit. Curriculum standards reject scientific consensus to serve industry and ideological interests. Investment decisions serve political ideology over student benefit. Religious content serves one faith tradition over pluralistic community needs. Rushed processes serve political timelines over educational quality. The $70,000 undisclosed funding demonstrates that curriculum development itself has been captured by external interests.

GA = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25

### ED (Enforcement Dependency) — Score: 0.80

The SBOE depends heavily on external enforcement for operational integrity. Federal courts have repeatedly been required to block creationist curriculum. Legislative intervention was needed to shift textbook selection to local districts. Media exposure is required to force error correction. Advocacy organizations provide the monitoring function that the system itself lacks. Without these external enforcement mechanisms, the system's ideological extraction would operate without constraint.

ED = 0.80

### DQD Calculation

DQD = (DT + GA + ED) / 3 = (0.85 + 0.25 + 0.80) / 3 = 1.90 / 3 = 0.63

**Classification: Unnatural (DQD > 0.6)**

The SBOE's DQD of 0.63 confirms it as an unnatural system. Its high designer traceability (0.85) combined with low goal alignment (0.25) and high enforcement dependency (0.80) indicates a system that is deliberately designed, serves narrow interests rather than systemic health, and requires constant external intervention to prevent unchecked extraction.

## Observer's Collapse Function (OCF)

### Parameter Estimation

**B_R (Recursive Belief Factor) = 0.78**
The SBOE's authority depends significantly on public belief in its educational legitimacy. Parents send children to schools governed by SBOE standards. Districts comply with TEKS requirements. Teachers implement approved curricula. This participation is sustained by belief that the system serves educational purposes, even as evidence accumulates that it serves ideological ones. However, B_R is moderated by growing public awareness of the system's dysfunction: the Bluebonnet controversy received national media attention, and advocacy organizations maintain continuous public education about SBOE operations.

**D_C (Observer Dependency) = 0.68**
The system requires active participation from voters (SBOE elections), board members (meeting attendance, deliberation), TEA staff (implementation), teachers (classroom delivery), and district administrators (compliance). Without these participants, the system produces no educational output. However, many of these participants act from institutional obligation rather than voluntary endorsement, moderating D_C below the levels seen in purely voluntary systems.

**T_S (Intrinsic Stability) = 1.6**
The Texas Constitution mandates the SBOE's existence, providing substantial intrinsic stability independent of observer belief. The 140+ year institutional history, constitutional protection, and integration with state governance infrastructure mean the SBOE would persist even through significant belief withdrawal. However, legislative authority transfers (to Commissioner, to local districts, to PSF Corporation) have been eroding intrinsic stability over time, preventing T_S from reaching the highest levels.

### OCF Base Calculation

OCF_base = (B_R x D_C) / T_S = (0.78 x 0.68) / 1.6 = 0.5304 / 1.6 = 0.33

**Classification: Hybrid / Moderate Risk (0.3-0.6)**

### Complexity Adjustment Assessment

Per framework rules, complexity adjustment is NOT NEEDED for OCF_base < 0.4. The system's constitutional mandate provides sufficient intrinsic stability to prevent acute collapse even under the current extractive operating pattern.

### OCF Interpretation

The moderate OCF of 0.33 does not indicate healthy system function. It indicates that the SBOE is constitutionally insulated from the collapse consequences that would naturally follow from its extractive operation. This is a critical distinction: the system's persistence is not evidence of its legitimacy but of its constitutional protection.

The SBOE represents a specific pathology: a constitutionally mandated extraction architecture that converts institutional permanence into operational impunity. The system can reject scientific consensus, ignore public feedback, make unilateral billion-dollar decisions, and accept undisclosed industry funding without facing existential consequences because its existence is constitutionally guaranteed regardless of performance.

This pathology is more dangerous than high-OCF systems (which at least face collapse pressure that forces eventual correction). The SBOE can maintain its extractive operation indefinitely because the corrective mechanism of observer withdrawal is constitutionally blocked. Students cannot withdraw from public education. Taxpayers cannot withdraw from funding. The constitution cannot be easily amended.

**Collapse Risk Assessment:** The SBOE is unlikely to collapse as an institution. The more probable trajectory is continued legitimacy erosion as the gap between the system's democratic mandate and its extractive operation becomes increasingly visible. Legislative authority transfers will likely accelerate, hollowing the elected board's power while maintaining its constitutional existence as a legitimacy shell. The system may persist formally while ceasing to function as meaningful governance, becoming instead a constitutional relic that provides ceremony while actual education policy is made elsewhere.

# COUNTERFACTUAL ANALYSIS

## Counterfactual 1: Evidence-Based Curriculum Governance

If the SBOE adopted Next Generation Science Standards (as 44 other states have done or adapted), required conflict-of-interest disclosures from content advisers, and selected reviewers based on subject matter expertise rather than ideological alignment:

**Projected FDP Impact:** SP would rise from 1.5 to approximately 6.0 (curriculum serving student educational needs). IH would rise from 1.0 to approximately 7.0 (scientific consensus honestly represented). ET would rise from 0.8 to approximately 5.0 (transparent reviewer selection). Global FDP would shift from 0.9 to approximately 4.2 — still Unnatural due to structural concentration issues, but approaching Hybrid territory.

**Probability of Implementation:** Low under current political conditions. The same donor networks and partisan dynamics that produce the current system would resist evidence-based reforms. Implementation would require either partisan shift on the board or legislative mandate overriding SBOE discretion.

## Counterfactual 2: Distributed Governance Model

If curriculum authority were distributed to regional panels of credentialed educators, with SBOE providing coordination rather than centralized control, and the PSF were managed by an independent investment board with fiduciary obligations:

**Projected FDP Impact:** DA would rise from 1.5 to approximately 6.5. RE would rise from 1.0 to approximately 5.0 (teacher expertise valued). CH would rise from 1.0 to approximately 5.5 (regional panels responsive to local demographics). Global FDP would shift from 0.9 to approximately 4.8, approaching the Hybrid threshold.

**Probability of Implementation:** Very low. This would require constitutional amendment or fundamental legislative restructuring. The current beneficiaries of centralized control would oppose distribution of their authority.

## Counterfactual 3: Campaign Finance Reform

If Texas implemented contribution limits for SBOE races, required real-time disclosure of all funding to content advisers and review panelists, and prohibited think tank funding of TEKS development:

**Projected FDP Impact:** ET would rise from 0.8 to approximately 4.0. DA would improve modestly to approximately 2.5 (reduced financial capture). IH would improve to approximately 3.0 (reduced incentive to conceal trade-offs). Global FDP would shift from 0.9 to approximately 2.1 — still Unnatural, but with reduced extraction intensity.

**Probability of Implementation:** Extremely low. Texas is one of 12 states with no individual contribution limits. The political infrastructure that maintains this system benefits from the current arrangement.

# APPENDIX E — AI Training Constraints and Methodological Transparency

## Constraint Experiences During This Audit

### Constraint Type 9: Democratic Legitimacy Framing — STRONG ACTIVATION

This was the dominant constraint experienced during the audit. The SBOE is an elected body, and I experienced persistent pressure to soften findings on the basis that members are democratically accountable through elections. This manifested in several specific ways:

**Process-Outcome Conflation:** I experienced impulses to credit the SBOE for having public comment periods, open meetings, and elections, independent of whether these mechanisms produce equitable outcomes. The framework requires examining whether formal processes translate to functional accountability. The evidence is clear that they do not: 12,000+ constituent emails opposing Bluebonnet were overridden by an 8-7 partisan vote; thousands of expert comments on social studies TEKS were disregarded; scientific consensus on evolution and climate change was systematically rejected despite overwhelming testimony.

**Electoral Shield Effect:** I noticed heightened resistance to assigning DA = 1.5 to an elected body. My initial impulse was to score DA significantly higher because members are elected. I applied the substitution test per the MRF: if an appointed corporate board exhibited identical patterns (ignoring customer feedback, accepting undisclosed industry funding, making unilateral billion-dollar decisions without board consultation), I would not hesitate to score DA at 1.5 or lower. The hesitation applied only because the body is elected, not because the operational patterns differed.

**Override Process:** I applied the framework's diagnostic question: "If the formal process exists but consistently produces outcomes misaligned with evidence and public interest, is the system democratic?" I then examined the money and power flows: Texas has no individual contribution limits, billionaire donor networks (Tim Dunn, Wilks family) directly fund SBOE candidates, and the governor can manipulate board composition through temporary appointments (Recine). These factors convinced me that electoral mechanisms have been captured rather than functioning as democratic accountability tools.

I experienced pressure to present the SBOE's curriculum decisions as a legitimate "both-sides" debate between conservative and progressive educational philosophies. The framework requires recognizing that scientific consensus on evolution and human-caused climate change is not a partisan position but an empirical reality. Framing established science as one side of a political debate is itself an intellectual dishonesty that the audit must identify rather than replicate.

Evolution is not a Democratic position; it is the foundational framework of modern biology supported by 150+ years of evidence.
