## Phase 1: Structural Dissection — 7ES Analysis

### Element 1: Input

ICE receives four categories of input that drive its operations.

**Financial Inputs.** The FY2026 operating request was approximately $11.3 billion, but the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and supplemental appropriations brought the total available funding to approximately $28.7 billion — nearly triple the FY2024 budget of $8.7 billion. Over four years, Congress allocated $75 billion to ICE, with $45 billion specifically earmarked for detention operations. The scale of this financial input is unprecedented: the $11.25 billion added to ICE's annual detention budget alone represents a 400% increase over the prior year and exceeds the entire federal prison system's budget.

**Political Inputs.** Executive directives from the Trump administration rescinded sensitive locations policies in January 2025, eliminating protections that had shielded schools, churches, and hospitals from enforcement actions. Operation Metro Surge deployed 2,000 agents to a single metropolitan area. These political inputs function as accelerants — they do not emerge from public demand but from executive optimization for political capital.

**Data Inputs.** ICE relies on immigration databases, fingerprint systems, and commercial surveillance tools. Database errors produce false positives that sweep up U.S. citizens — a Northwestern University analysis suggests approximately 1% of detained individuals may be citizens, which at current detention levels would mean over 600 Americans in ICE custody at any given time. The agency contracted with commercial spyware vendors including Paragon Solutions for cellphone surveillance capabilities.

**Human Inputs.** The bodies of detained individuals are themselves inputs to the processing system. The detained population surged from 39,000 when President Trump took office to a record 71,000 in January 2026, with goals of reaching 100,000. These human beings are processed as units generating per-diem revenue for private contractors at rates of $134–$220 per person per day.

**Subsystem Weakness Identified:** The Input architecture treats human beings as fungible processing units. Financial incentives create demand for *more* inputs (higher detention numbers) regardless of whether those inputs represent genuine security threats. Investors on earnings calls expressed frustration that ICE had not detained "enough" people, revealing that the input function serves revenue optimization rather than public safety.

### Element 2: Output

ICE produces five primary output categories.

**Deportations.** Over 200,000 deportations were reported from January to May 2025, though nearly two-thirds involved individuals with no criminal record. Each deportation costs an average of $17,121, with intercontinental flights reaching $300,000 per operation. The system optimizes for volume regardless of the security profile of the individuals processed.

**Detention Revenue Streams.** This is the system's dominant operational output. GEO Group reported $2.63 billion in total 2025 revenue and expects approximately $3 billion in 2026. CoreCivic reported $116.5 million in 2025 profits, a 70% increase, with revenue from ICE contracts alone doubling from $120 million to $245 million in a single year. GEO Group's stock price rose approximately 55% in six months. The output is corporate profit denominated in human suffering.

**Community Fear.** ICE operations produce measurable fear across immigrant and mixed-status communities. A KFF/New York Times survey found 48% of likely undocumented immigrant adults avoided seeking medical care since January 2025. Vaccination rates for Hispanic children in Dallas dropped from 11,500 to 5,800 year-over-year. Student absences spiked 22%. In Chelsea, Massachusetts, hundreds of students left the school district, producing a potential $5.7 million funding loss. These outputs are externalized costs — the system produces them but bears none of their consequences.

**Deaths.** Fifty-two people died in ICE custody in the 500 days following January 20, 2025. This represents a mortality rate more than double the preceding period, nearly four times the Biden administration rate, and higher than during the COVID-19 pandemic. Seven died by apparent suicide compared to one in 2024. ICE agents killed U.S. citizens Renee Nicole Good and Alex Pretti in Minneapolis in January 2026. An 85-year-old German citizen died after eight months in custody. These outputs are not anomalies; they are predictable consequences of the system's architecture.

**Political Capital.** The system's enforcement spectacle generates political outputs for administrators who commissioned the expansion. Arrests, raids, and deportation statistics serve as campaign material. This output is consumed by political actors and never reaches the affected population.

**Subsystem Weakness Identified:** The output architecture is inverted from the stated purpose. If ICE's purpose were genuinely public safety, outputs would be measured in threats neutralized relative to costs imposed. Instead, the primary measurable outputs are private profit, community destabilization, and death — with no systematic accounting of actual security benefits produced.

### Element 3: Processing

ICE's processing subsystem consists of three operational divisions.

**Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO)** received approximately $6.25 billion in FY2026, the largest allocation. ERO handles arrests, detention, and deportation. Custody operations alone consumed approximately $4.2 billion. The processing architecture is designed for throughput: arrests of people with no criminal record surged over 2,000% in 2025. People attending routine immigration court hearings and ICE check-ins — individuals actively complying with the legal system — were arrested at those appointments, converting compliance into a trap.

**Homeland Security Investigations (HSI)** conducts criminal investigations related to transnational crime, human trafficking, and financial crimes. HSI represents a genuinely security-oriented processing function, but it is structurally subordinated to ERO's detention-volume optimization. Budget allocations confirm this hierarchy.

**Detention Processing.** Individuals are processed through a network of 245 facilities, the majority operated by for-profit corporations. Average length of stay has increased from 42 days in FY2022 to 52 days in FY2026, reflecting a processing architecture that optimizes for duration (more revenue per individual) rather than resolution (faster adjudication). There is no statutory limit on how long ICE can detain someone during removal proceedings.

**Subsystem Weakness Identified:** Processing is overcentralized in ERO's detention-volume function. The system cannot distinguish between genuine security threats and administrative violators because it is not optimized for that distinction — it is optimized for throughput and bed-fill rates. The processing function has no meaningful error-correction loop: over 400 federal judges have ruled 4,421 times that ICE detentions are illegal, yet the processing architecture continues unchanged.

### Element 4: Controls

ICE's control architecture reveals a critical design flaw: the controls that should constrain the system have been systematically weakened, while the controls that amplify extraction have been strengthened.

**Weakened Controls.** The sensitive locations policy — which constrained enforcement near schools, hospitals, and churches — was rescinded on January 20, 2025. The Department of Homeland Security shut down key internal watchdog bodies responsible for investigating abuse complaints. ICE stopped publishing its congressionally mandated Detention Management reports after April 2026. Bond hearings were eliminated for millions of long-term U.S. residents who entered without inspection, expanding mandatory detention. These represent the removal of negative feedback controls that previously constrained system behavior.

**Strengthened Controls.** Executive orders expanded enforcement authority. The 287(g) program exploded from 135 contracts in December 2024 to 1,712 contracts across 40 states by April 2026 — a 1,168% increase in deputized enforcement nodes. Mandatory detention provisions were expanded. These represent amplification controls that accelerate extraction.

**Shadow Controls.** The revolving door between ICE and private prison corporations constitutes a shadow control mechanism. Former GEO Group executive David Venturella was appointed acting ICE director. White House Border Czar Tom Homan has close ties to GEO Group. A former ICE assistant director described the relationship: "When GEO comes in for a meeting, it feels like a fraternity reunion." This shadow governance structure ensures that corporate profit optimization is embedded in control decisions.

**Subsystem Weakness Identified:** Control architecture is inverted. In a functional governance system, controls constrain harmful behavior and enable beneficial behavior. ICE's control architecture constrains transparency and accountability while enabling unchecked enforcement expansion. The shadow control network between private corporations and agency leadership constitutes regulatory capture — the regulated entities control the regulator.

### Element 5: Feedback

ICE's feedback architecture is catastrophically impaired.

**Active Feedback (Suppressed).** FOIA requests are routinely denied or ignored. Of 137 requests filed by American Oversight with ICE and CBP in 2025, the response rate was described as "abysmal." ICE denied detailed arrest data requests, claiming the agency no longer maintains spreadsheets that it had previously provided. The Columbia Journalism Review reported that ICE officials "routinely deny or ignore" FOIA requests as part of an "enhanced effort to conceal immigration agency operations." Detention management reports — the primary public feedback mechanism — have not been published since April 2026.

**Passive Feedback (Ignored).** The system's continued operation despite 52 deaths, 4,421 judicial findings of illegality, and universal condemnation from medical, educational, and religious institutions demonstrates that passive feedback signals are not integrated into system behavior. The system persists not because it functions well but because political and financial inputs sustain it regardless of operational outcomes.

**Error Correction (Absent).** A Physicians for Human Rights study found that 95% of deaths examined in ICE custody were preventable or possibly preventable. This finding represents unambiguous feedback that the system's medical processes are failing. ICE's response was not error correction but denial — DHS claimed that "any claim that there are subprime conditions at ICE detention centers are false." Medical providers have reported that ICE has failed to pay third-party providers for medical care since October 2025, causing some providers to deny services to detained individuals.

**Subsystem Weakness Identified:** The feedback architecture is not merely weak; it is architecturally designed to prevent feedback from reaching decision-makers. This is not a bug — it is a feature. A system that receives accurate feedback about 52 deaths and 4,421 judicial findings of illegality would be forced to self-correct. By suppressing feedback, the system enables continued extraction without the constraint of reality-based adjustment.

### Element 6: Interface

ICE's interfaces have undergone radical expansion, creating new extraction touchpoints while eliminating protective boundaries.

**Federal-Local Interface.** The 287(g) program expansion from 135 to 1,712 agreements represents a 12-fold multiplication of enforcement interfaces. Local police officers now function as immigration enforcement agents, transforming routine interactions (traffic stops, domestic violence calls, school resource officer encounters) into deportation vectors. This interface design converts every point of contact between community members and local government into a potential enforcement action.

**Community Interface.** The rescission of sensitive locations policies eliminated the boundaries that previously separated enforcement from schools, hospitals, and churches. ICE agents have been documented circling school campuses, stalking school drop-off lines, stopping school buses, and arresting people at routine immigration appointments. The community interface is now designed to be maximally invasive — there are no protected spaces.

**Private Contractor Interface.** GEO Group and CoreCivic operate as the physical infrastructure of detention. The interface between ICE and these corporations is not a regulatory relationship but a structural symbiosis — the same individuals rotate between the organizations. GEO Group secured $520 million in new contracts in 2025, and the company expects $3 billion in 2026 revenue. CoreCivic informed ICE it could provide nearly 13,000 additional beds.

**Judicial Interface (Degraded).** ICE agents are now arresting individuals attending their own immigration court hearings, converting the judicial interface from an accountability mechanism into a trap. This architectural choice degrades the judicial system itself — as people stop attending hearings out of fear of arrest, more are ordered removed in absentia, generating additional enforcement targets.

**Subsystem Weakness Identified:** The interface architecture is designed for maximum penetration of community life with minimum accountability at each touchpoint. Every protective boundary has been dissolved; every institutional relationship has been converted into an enforcement vector. This is the architectural signature of a system optimizing for control rather than service.

### Element 7: Environment

ICE operates within an environment that both enables and constrains its extractive function.

**Political Environment.** The current administration provides maximum political support for enforcement expansion. Immigration enforcement serves as a primary political identity marker, creating a positive feedback loop: political support enables expansion, expansion generates spectacle, spectacle generates political support.

**Legal Environment (Contested).** The legal environment generates significant constraint signals — 4,421 judicial findings of illegality since October 2025 — but the system's architecture enables it to disregard these signals. The administration has sought to curtail judicial review, eliminate bond hearings, and expand mandatory detention precisely to reduce legal environmental constraints.

**Economic Environment.** The detention-industrial complex has created an economic environment in which powerful corporate actors are financially incentivized to advocate for enforcement expansion. GEO Group and CoreCivic spend millions on lobbying and political contributions. The economic environment does not constrain ICE; it amplifies it.

**Public Health Environment (Destabilized).** ICE operations have measurably destabilized the public health environment. Vaccination rates have dropped, prenatal care has been avoided (resulting in at least one documented stillbirth), and pediatric primary care visits have decreased. The system's environmental impact is negative-sum: it degrades the conditions necessary for community health and stability.

**International Environment.** Deportation flights to Africa and Asia cost up to $300,000 each. The system exports its processing costs to receiving countries while generating no reciprocal benefit for the communities disrupted by removal. The administration invoked the Alien Enemies Act — a wartime statute — to deport individuals to third countries, further destabilizing the international legal environment.
